# Executed scope - revision 0.2.0

The package preserves the original audited source concepts and adds comparative theory and experiments. All numerical observations are synthetic. Original private notebooks are not required for reproduction and are not republished in full.

## Delivered

A revised modular manuscript and full supplement; 51-record source ledger; analytical financing, coverage, equity and incidence arguments; matched-levy equivalence; 549 macro paths comprising the original 450 plus 99 additional comparative paths; 15 vector figures; generated CSV and LaTeX tables; an Excel companion including comparisons; a pinned Python repository; tests; SCED editorial materials; and a long-form explanatory article.

The added paths cover stronger capital taxation, targeted support and matched dividend levies. The tax tilt is a scenario, not an optimal tax estimate. The matched levy deliberately uses the same investment and participation rules as equity; its equality is an invariance test, not an empirical discovery that actual institutions behave alike.

## Not delivered or established

No empirical national estimation, full banking/public-debt/external-sector model, endogenous market equity valuation, causal participant-effort estimate, validated real-world contribution scoring, estimated corporate-entry response, deployment-ready digital currency, formal securities contract, public repository, registered DOI, authorised open-source licence or actual journal submission. The literature is bounded and some records are abstract-only.

Universal reference-quantile support is not a guaranteed individual minimum. Targeted predicted-gap support also has ex-post errors. Long-horizon paths contain no demographic turnover or endogenous firm entry. They are scenarios, not forecasts. Book-wealth comparisons omit capitalised future fiscal claims.

## Main conclusion

Usownership can reduce future transfers under the stated award and dividend rules, but well-designed levy/rebate contracts can reproduce its cash flows. Any extra merit must rest on measured differences in rights, participation, governance, liquidity or commitment. Monetary financing remains bounded and complements fiscal policy in the stable central scenario.
